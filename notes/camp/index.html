Routing specialists instead of taking a majority vote

CAMP · ACL 2026 Student Research Workshop · third author

A fixed panel of experts and a majority vote treats every clinical case the same. The panel does not change when the case does, and a weak majority still decides.

CAMP builds the panel per case. An attending agent picks the specialists that case needs. They vote, and a router then does one of three things: accept a real consensus, fall back to the attending, or arbitrate from the evidence when the panel disagrees.

I implemented that routing and arbitration, and the evaluation around it. We ran four language models on 2,000+ MIMIC-IV cases. Macro F1 was 87.5, which is 5.3 points above the baseline.
